================================================================================
PART 1 — IS THE CORE THESIS LOGICALLY SOUND?
================================================================================

THE THESIS, RESTATED
--------------------
"Don't try to win the speed race. Model EV of the next game-state evolution,
combine it with execution probability (p_fill), trade only when expected
realized EV is positive after fill risk and microstructure effects."

This thesis is logically defensible for our position in the market.

WHY IT IS DEFENSIBLE
--------------------
1. Honest about competitive position. We are not co-located with Polygon,
   we are not running an FPGA pipeline against the StatsAPI websocket, and
   we have no reason to believe we can beat informed reaction-traders to a
   scoring event. Refusing to compete on a dimension where we lose is
   correct. Many retail strategies fail because they enter the speed race
   they cannot win.

2. Selection over reaction. The bot is structured as a selective predicate
   ("only bet when X conditions hold AND model EV > threshold"), not a
   reactive one ("bet whenever ask jumps"). Selection is the right battle
   for a slow participant.

3. Two-stage probability decomposition. Splitting the decision into
   p_win_if_filled and p_fill, then optimizing the product, is the correct
   framework for a market with adverse selection. Most amateur sports
   models stop at p_win and ignore that the trades they get filled on are
   a non-random subset of the trades they tried to place.

WHERE THE THESIS HAS HIDDEN ASSUMPTIONS
---------------------------------------
1. We assume the historical Poisson cache (Stage-1) generalizes. It is built
   on 5 years of MLB. If 2026 has a different run environment, ball, strike
   zone, or reliever usage pattern, the cache decays. We have not yet built
   automated drift detection.

2. We assume park/weather (Stage-2) and team offense (Stage-3) corrections
   are unbiased. A logistic residual fit on the same training distribution
   can produce well-calibrated marginal predictions but biased conditional
   ones. Our gates partly compensate (e.g., S2 extreme suppression) but the
   compensation is hand-tuned, not learned.

3. We assume Polymarket O/U markets are "beatable in selective regimes" —
   i.e., that there exist subsets of game states where market price is
   slower or noisier than our model. The data (counterfactual win rate
   = 80%) suggests this is true on signal generation. But the data also
   shows the orders we get FILLED on have a 54.5% win rate, which means
   the market is selectively filling us on its informed side. This is the
   single most important empirical finding in the project so far.


================================================================================
PART 2 — GAME-THEORETIC EVALUATION
================================================================================

WHO IS ON THE OTHER SIDE OF OUR TRADES?
---------------------------------------
On Polymarket MLB O/U markets, the counterparties placing the asks we lift
are some mix of:

  (a) Market makers quoting both sides for spread capture.
  (b) Informed traders who saw the same StatsAPI update we did, faster.
  (c) Other models with similar but not identical FV estimates.
  (d) Hedgers offloading correlated positions.
  (e) Noise traders / casual bettors.

Our profitability requires that, on average, we are trading against
(a), (c), (d), and (e) — and NOT (b).

THE WINNER'S-CURSE FINGERPRINT IS ALREADY IN OUR DATA
-----------------------------------------------------
From the evidence snapshot:

   Filled win rate:                        54.5%  (6W / 5L)
   Cancelled-bet counterfactual win rate:  94.7%  (18W / 1L)
   Gap:                                    40.2 percentage points

This 40-point gap is not random. It is the textbook fingerprint of adverse
selection. The orders that get filled are disproportionately the ones the
market wanted to sell us (because the market was right). The orders that
get cancelled are disproportionately the ones nobody wanted to sell at our
price (because we were right and the market knew it too).

In game-theoretic terms: when we post a limit BUY at price p, we are
offering a put option to anyone with information about the next 5 seconds
of game state. Informed sellers exercise that option (sell to us) only
when they expect price to move down — i.e., when our bet is more likely
to lose. Uninformed sellers exercise it whenever it crosses their book.
The mix of informed-vs-uninformed sellers determines our edge.

This is why "edge > 0.25" filled bets are 0W/5L: a 25-point gap between
our model and the market is a near-certain signal that the market knows
something our cache does not (typically a phantom-run case where we
inferred a score that did not happen, or a regime our Poisson cache does
not represent). The market correctly fills those orders because we are
the uninformed counterparty.

The TR12/TR13 gates (FV saturation, large FV/ask gap, blowout-adjacent,
S2 extreme suppression) are not arbitrary risk filters. They are
gate-level acknowledgements of adverse selection in specific regimes.

GAME-THEORETIC IMPLICATIONS FOR THE THESIS
------------------------------------------
1. Our alpha is bounded by what informed sellers do not know. As any
   informed counterparty's model improves, our edge in that regime
   compresses. We are competing against a moving target.

2. Cancel rate is informative, not just operational. A high cancel rate
   in a given regime where the cancelled-counterfactual win rate is high
   means we are correctly avoiding adverse selection. Counterintuitively,
   higher fill rates would be BAD if they came from filling more in the
   regimes the market knows we're wrong in.

3. The right metric is not fill rate. It is the win-rate gap between
   filled and cancelled. If we can shrink that gap from 40pp toward 0pp
   while keeping signal quality up, we have moved up the food chain.

4. Execution policy IS the alpha. Not the FV model. The FV model
   identifies candidate regimes; the execution policy decides whether
   we can trade them without being adversely selected. Roadmap item #6
   (learned execution policy) is the most leveraged work item in the
   project.

PART 3 — STATISTICAL EVALUATION OF PROFITABILITY PATH
================================================================================

CURRENT EMPIRICAL POSITION
--------------------------
Through 2026-04-22 (per README evidence snapshot):
   Live signals:                31
   Settled fills:                11    (54.5% fill rate)
   Filled W-L:                  6W-5L  (54.5% win rate)
   Counterfactual W-L (all):    24/30  (80.0% on signals)
   Realized P&L:               -$70.72

Edge-bucket split (the most actionable finding):
   edge ≤ 0.25 filled:    6W / 1L   (85.7% WR, +20.5% ROI)
   edge >  0.25 filled:   0W / 5L   (   0% WR, -100% ROI)

Plus the most recent live session (post-P0 fixes): ~3W / 2L (60% WR).

BREAK-EVEN MATH
---------------
Average filled limit ≈ 0.65 (typical "ask floor 0.55, gate up to ~0.75").
At limit 0.65, payout if won = 1 / 0.65 = 1.538x stake. Break-even
win rate = 0.65 = 65%.

Therefore:
   54.5% filled WR → unprofitable by ~10pp.
   60.0% recent WR → still unprofitable by ~5pp.
   85.7% edge≤0.25 WR → profitable by ~20pp (consistent with +20.5% ROI).

The edge≤0.25 bucket is the only subset of our data that clears
break-even. Whether that subset is real or noise is the central
statistical question.

SAMPLE-SIZE REALITY CHECK
-------------------------
Standard error on a binomial proportion at n=7, p=0.857 is:
   SE = sqrt(0.857 * 0.143 / 7) ≈ 13.2%
   95% CI ≈ [0.60, 1.00]  (one-tailed Wilson)

Translation: the "85.7% edge≤0.25 win rate" is consistent with anything
from "barely break-even" to "obvious money printer". We literally do not
have enough data yet to distinguish those hypotheses.

For 95% CI half-width of ±5pp on the edge≤0.25 subset at p=0.80, we need
roughly n ≈ 246 fills in that bucket. At ~1 such fill per session, that
is ~250 sessions = ~1.5 MLB seasons.

For ±10pp half-width: ~62 fills. ~2 months at current pace.

The minimum credible validation point is around 30 fills in the
edge≤0.25 bucket — enough to push the lower bound of the win-rate CI
above the ~65% break-even line. We are at 7. We need ~23 more.

VARIANCE / DRAWDOWN UNDER CURRENT SIZING
----------------------------------------
At quarter-Kelly with edge≤0.25 cap, daily-budget $125, max single bet
fraction 0.33 (~$41):
   Per-bet variance is dominated by the limit price, not the stake.
   At ~$13 avg stake, single-bet downside ≈ $13, upside ≈ $7.
   Worst plausible weekly drawdown at ~5 bets/week and 50% WR:
     -5 * $13 = -$65, ~3% of a $2,000 bankroll.

Sizing is appropriately defensive. We will not blow up. We will also not
generate enough P&L to be statistically significant for several months.
This is by design and it is the correct posture given the sample size.


================================================================================
PART 4 — IS THERE A PATH TO LONG-TERM PROFITABILITY?
================================================================================

HONEST ANSWER: PROBABLY YES, BUT NOT YET PROVEN.

The strong points:
  + The thesis is logically defensible and game-theoretically aware.
  + The infrastructure is now mature: refactored modules, shadow
    metrics, EV policy scaffold, candidate-universe data capture.
  + The single most credible profitability signal in the data
    (edge≤0.25 → 85.7% WR / +20.5% ROI) is consistent with a real
    edge in the regime where our model and market roughly agree
    but our gates filter out the adversely-selected fills.
  + Gate evolution (TR1–TR13) shows disciplined, evidence-driven
    iteration. The current gate stack reflects a real understanding
    of failure modes, not curve-fitting.
  + Conservative sizing means we can survive a long validation phase
    without bankroll-ending drawdowns.

The honest concerns:
  - Filled win rate (54.5%) is below break-even. The strategy is
    losing money in aggregate today.
  - The "edge≤0.25 is profitable" finding is built on n=7. Statistically
    this is a hint, not a confirmation.
  - The 40pp Winner's-Curse gap is a structural problem, not a tuning
    problem. It requires execution policy changes (Roadmap #6), not
    just gate changes.
  - We have no automated drift detection on Stage-1/2/3. A regime
    change in MLB run environment (e.g., a mid-season ball change)
    would silently invalidate the cache.
  - Polymarket counterparty quality is unknown. As the venue grows,
    the share of informed counterparties will rise, compressing edge.

NET ASSESSMENT
--------------
The model is in a "promising but unvalidated" state. The thesis is right.
The implementation is competent. The data is too thin to claim
profitability with confidence. The most likely outcome of the next ~60
sessions is:

   Scenario A (~55% likely): edge≤0.25 holds at 70-80% WR. The model
     is profitable at small scale (~$100-300/month at current sizing).
     Validation is achieved around 30-50 fills. Sizing can then ramp.

   Scenario B (~30% likely): edge≤0.25 regresses to 60-65% WR as
     sample grows. The model is roughly break-even. Either execution
     policy improvements (Roadmap #6) push it back into profit, or
     the project ends as a sophisticated learning exercise.

   Scenario C (~15% likely): something we have not measured (e.g.,
     informed-counterparty share growing on Polymarket, or a Stage-1
     cache regime change) erodes edge faster than we can adapt. The
     model becomes consistently unprofitable and we conclude the
     market is no longer beatable from our position.

This is roughly the realistic distribution. None of them is "obvious
money machine"; none of them is "definitely doomed".


================================================================================
PART 5 — NEXT STEPS, IN PRIORITY ORDER
================================================================================

TIER 1 — VALIDATION (do these now; no model changes required)
-------------------------------------------------------------
1. KEEP CURRENT GATE/THRESHOLD STACK STABLE FOR ~30 MORE FILLS.
   Do not tune gates based on individual session outcomes. The gates
   were set on a thesis; they need a sample to test against. Changing
   them mid-stream destroys the only validation experiment we have
   running.

2. TRACK EDGE-BUCKET WIN RATE SEPARATELY IN EVERY SESSION REPORT.
   The edge≤0.25 vs edge>0.25 split is the primary thesis-test. Every
   session report should explicitly report the running tally and the
   one-sided lower-bound CI on edge≤0.25 win rate. We are testing one
   hypothesis; the metric should be in our face every day.

3. CONTINUE SHADOW METRICS AND CANDIDATE-UNIVERSE LOGGING.
   These produce the data we need for Tier 2. No changes; just keep
   the pipeline running cleanly.

TIER 2 — EXECUTION POLICY (the actual alpha lever)
--------------------------------------------------
4. SHIP THE EV POLICY IN SHADOW MODE.
   The LogisticJsonScorer + backtest_ev_policy infrastructure is in
   place. Run it in shadow alongside the current rule for ~30 sessions.
   Compare: would EV policy have shifted any borderline trades from
   trade→no-trade or vice versa, and would those shifts have improved
   realized PnL? This is roadmap item #5/#10 and is currently the
   highest-leverage active work item.

5. BUILD A FILL-MODEL FEATURE: queue position proxy.
   The Winner's-Curse gap is execution-side. The single most useful
   new feature would be a proxy for "how aggressive would we need to
   be to actually fill this order in the next N seconds vs how much
   does that aggression cost us in terms of being filled by informed
   sellers". Even a coarse proxy (ask velocity, depth at touch,
   recent fill density) would help.

6. EXPERIMENT WITH LIMIT PRICING POLICY IN A CONTROLLED A/B.
   Once shadow EV is stable, the next experiment is varying
   spread_factor (currently 0.65) by regime. The hypothesis: in
   regimes where cancelled counterfactual win rate is highest, we
   should be more aggressive (push limit toward ask). In regimes
   where filled win rate is closest to cancelled, we are already
   pricing efficiently. Start small: two values, run 20 sessions.

TIER 3 — DRIFT AND RISK INFRASTRUCTURE
--------------------------------------
7. ADD STAGE-1 CACHE DRIFT MONITORING.
   Compare 2026-to-date scoring distribution by inning vs the
   2021-2025 cache base. A simple KS-test or chi-square per
   inning_state cell, run weekly. Alert when drift exceeds threshold.
   Without this, we cannot tell when a structural cache update is
   needed.

8. ADD CALIBRATION RELIABILITY DIAGRAMS PER EDGE BUCKET.
   We claim p_win_if_filled is well-calibrated. Verify it. Reliability
   diagrams (predicted bucket vs realized win rate) are the standard
   tool. Run monthly once we have ≥30 fills.

9. DOCUMENT A KILL-SWITCH TRIGGER POLICY.
   At what realized cumulative loss do we stop live trading and
   re-evaluate? A pre-committed rule (e.g., -$300 cumulative loss
   triggers a 1-week pause + full diagnostic review) prevents
   plan-revision under emotional pressure. Write it down before we
   need it.

TIER 4 — DEFER UNTIL VALIDATED
------------------------------
10. SCALE STAKE. Not now. Quarter-Kelly + caps is correct for the
    current sample size. Revisit only after edge≤0.25 win rate has
    a one-sided 95% lower bound above ~70% with n≥30.

11. MULTI-LINE / CORRELATED EXPOSURE OPTIMIZATION. Currently capped
    by per-game-budget-fraction. That is sufficient until validated.

12. REPLACING THE 3-STAGE FV MODEL WITH A LEARNED END-TO-END MODEL.
    Tempting but premature. The current model is interpretable and
    debuggable. A learned model would obscure the failure modes that
    the gates currently address. Wait until we have 200+ fills and
    can tell the new model apart from the old one statistically.


================================================================================
SUMMARY
================================================================================

The thesis ("model selection over reaction speed; trade only when realized
EV after fill risk is positive") is correct for our competitive position.
The infrastructure to test it is now built. The data so far hints that the
thesis is real in the edge≤0.25 regime but does not yet prove it.

The most important things we can do in the next 4-8 weeks are:

  1. Stop tuning. Let the current configuration generate a sample.
  2. Get the EV policy running in shadow.
  3. Track edge-bucket performance every session.
  4. Address the Winner's-Curse gap via execution-policy work, not
     more gates.

If edge≤0.25 win rate holds above 70% across the next 30 fills, we are
on a real path to long-term profitability. If it regresses below 65%,
the project becomes a research exercise unless execution policy changes
recover the gap.

We are at the validation stage of a serious project. We are not yet at
the "it works" stage. The next 60 sessions will tell us which one we are
heading toward.
