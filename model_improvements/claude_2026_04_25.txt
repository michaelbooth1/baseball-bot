Day 3 Analysis — 2026-04-24
Three bugs conspired to waste the only signal of the day
Signal: DET@CIN O8.5, inning 6 bottom, 0 outs, score 5-2 (DET leading). FV=0.968, edge=17.8%. Game ended 8-9 = 17 runs total — over by 8.5 runs.

The execution chain failed at every step:

Bug A — _compute_stake read live_args.stake but --stake is a trade_arg. live_args.stake doesn't exist → falls through to DEFAULT_LIVE_STAKE = 25.0. The bet was sized at $25 instead of $10.

Bug B — place_limit_buy(size=stake) but the actual parameter is size_usdc. Python raised TypeError before the order reached the CLOB.

Bug C — The exception wasn't caught. The bet remained in self._bets with order_status="pending" and stake=$25, locking $25 of the $30 budget. The remaining $5 was below any stake minimum, so all 11 subsequent signals hit budget_exhausted and were blocked for the rest of the session.

All three are fixed above: size_usdc, read trade_args.stake, wrap the CLOB call in try/except and mark "error" on failure (releasing the reserved budget).

Finding 1 — LTP=0.13 with ask=0.79 is a warning sign, but for a different reason than expected
The LTP (last traded price) was 0.13 — 13 cents — while the ask was 0.79. That's a 66-cent gap, the largest we've seen. Our new LTP logging correctly surfaced this in the LIVE BET line.

What happened: DET@CIN at Great American Ball Park started as a low-over-probability game (DET leading 5-2 with CIN needing more runs). The Polymarket market probably hadn't been traded actively at high prices before the inning-6 signal. The LTP=0.13 reflects an early-game trade when the over was unlikely, not a phantom run. By inning 6 with 7 runs scored and 1.5 more needed in 3+ half-innings, 0.79 is the correct ask.

This reveals a gap in our LTP interpretation: the LTP adversarial-selection signal (from our earlier analysis) is only meaningful when LTP is recent. A stale LTP from early innings is irrelevant to the current market state. We should add a "LTP staleness" concept — if LTP < ask - 0.30, it's probably stale and shouldn't influence the decision. For now, log a note when this condition occurs.

The signal itself was correct (FV=0.968, game went to 17 total). The LTP gap was noise, not signal.

Finding 2 — EV policy shadow shows 36% block rate (63/175)
The EV policy in shadow mode evaluated 175 signals and would have blocked 63 (36%). This is a high block rate. Combined with the correct outcome (DET@CIN would have won), this continues the pattern from Day 2 where the EV policy is too aggressive at inning 6.

The 175 evaluations being much higher than the 111 deduped candidate records confirms the EV policy fires before the budget/dedup checks. Many of the same game-state signals are evaluated multiple times per polling cycle (the dedup only suppresses the candidate JSONL write, not the EV evaluation).
