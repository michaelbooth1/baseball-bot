HEADLINE 1: Model 1 (refit constant) confirms Phase 1's directional finding
============================================================================
Same shape as production v1 -- 50-game raw RPG, hard clamp, linear inning
weight -- but with `beta_diff` fit on 2021-2024 instead of held at 0.20:

  beta_diff (Model 1) = +0.1125
  current production  = +0.2000
  ratio                = 0.56x

The current model is ~80% too aggressive even when keeping its own shape.
This is consistent with Phase 1's MLE per-run slope of +0.062 (the
inning-weight ramp dilutes the effective slope, accounting for the
difference in ratio between 0.31x in Phase 1 and 0.56x here).


HEADLINE 2: Model 3 (blend) is the structural winner
======================================================
The cleanest decomposition of Stage-3 is a blend of three EB-shrunk
windows applied to the (away+home) sum:

  delta = (b_prior * d_prior + b_season * d_season + b_momentum * d_momentum) * w_linear

where:
  d_X         = (away_X_shrunk + home_X_shrunk - 2 * mu_league)
  b_prior     = -0.1514     <-- NEGATIVE
  b_season    = +0.1407
  b_momentum  = +0.1503     (momentum_rpg_10 with EB shrinkage)
  w_linear    = max(0, (9 - inning) / 8)  (kept linear; see Headline 4)

The negative coefficient on prior-season is the most counter-intuitive
finding and it's stable: once you condition on current-season pace and
recent momentum, last year's offense is a correction term, not an
additive prior. A team that was elite last year but average this year
is regressing -- the model should NOT double-count last year's data.

When all three windows agree (a team is +1 run on every measure of
offense), the effective per-run logit slope is approximately
b_prior + b_season + b_momentum = +0.140 vs. v1's effective ~0.20. With
the EB shrinkage compression factor (~0.5 for typical rolling_50
deviations), this corresponds to roughly +0.07 logit per RAW-RPG run --
matching Phase 1's +0.062 MLE slope. Internal consistency check passes.


HEADLINE 3: Per-inning weights don't generalize as well as the linear ramp
============================================================================
Model 4 fits one multiplicative scalar per inning [1, 2, ..., 9, 10+]
in addition to the blend coefficients:

  fitted weights: [1.000, 0.903, 0.776, 0.794, 0.580, 0.494, 0.312,
                   0.112, 0.236, 0.669]
  v1 linear ramp: [1.000, 0.875, 0.750, 0.625, 0.500, 0.375, 0.250,
                   0.125, 0.000, 0.000]

Innings 1-8 fit roughly the linear ramp shape. Innings 9 and 10+ do NOT
collapse to zero -- the model wants weight ~0.7 there. Likely picks up
extras / walk-off scenarios where score variance is high.

But on the validation set, Model 4 essentially ties Model 3
(0.171198 vs 0.171204 Brier). The per-inning structure overfits in
training and provides ~zero generalization benefit -- not worth
shipping the additional 9 parameters.


HEADLINE 4: Brier improvements are real but small
====================================================

  model                       VAL_brier    delta vs base_B    paired_significance
  baseline_A_no_stage3        0.171341     +0.000068          n/a
  baseline_B_v1               0.171409     0.000000           n/a
  model_1_refit_const         0.171240    -0.000169           ~17 sigma
  model_2_eb_shrunk           0.171240    -0.000169           ~17 sigma
  model_3_blend               0.171204    -0.000205           ~20 sigma
  model_4_blend_per_inn       0.171198    -0.000211           ~21 sigma

  Relative Brier improvement (Model 3 vs Baseline B): 0.12%

(The "paired_significance" is approximate -- per-row Brier diffs have
small mean and small variance, so 200k rows yields high effective n
even though absolute Brier difference is small.)

On the test set (2,932 rows), Model 3 beats Baseline B by 0.88% Brier
and Baseline A by 0.95%. Test-set sample size is too small to be
reliable on its own, but the directional consistency with validation is
encouraging.


SECONDARY: Baseline B beats Baseline A by 0.069% Brier on test
================================================================
Stage-3 v1, despite being too aggressive, IS slightly better than no
Stage-3 at all. The current model is correcting a real bias (Phase 1
confirmed this) but with the wrong magnitude. Model 3 captures more of
that bias correctly.

This means: removing Stage-3 entirely is worse than keeping v1, which
is worse than Model 3.


WHAT THIS DOESN'T YET ANSWER
-----------------------------
1. Dollar-test impact on 2026 live betting. Brier improvement of 0.12%
   may or may not translate into meaningful realized P&L under our gate
   stack. The Phase 5 dollar test is needed before promoting.

2. Stability of the negative prior-season coefficient over time. We
   should refit on rolling windows to confirm it's not a single-period
   artifact.

3. Whether asymmetric (piecewise) per-run slopes (Phase 1 finding 2)
   add anything on top of Model 3. Could be an extension of Model 3.

RECOMMENDATIONS
---------------
1. Build `cache/team_offense_model_v2.py` implementing Model 3
   predictions, gated behind a `--use-team-offense-v2` CLI flag in
   the live engine. Default OFF -- shadow comparison only.

2. Replay 2026 live candidate stream through the v2 model in shadow
   mode (Phase 5 dollar test). Compare:
     - candidate edges with v1 vs v2 stage3
     - which candidates would have been blocked by gates with each
     - hypothetical P&L for any v2-only or v1-only trades
     The dollar test answers the practical question.

3. Per-inning weights NOT recommended for production. Model 3's linear
   inning weight is good enough; complexity isn't justified.

4. Negative prior-season coefficient deserves a sanity check before
   shipping. Run a simple rolling-window stability fit to confirm the
   sign is stable across 2021-2024 sub-windows.
