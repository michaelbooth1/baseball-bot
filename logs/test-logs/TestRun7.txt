  Inning 5 is the "bullpen transition" inning. Starting pitchers who survive
  through inning 4 are often pulled after inning 5 (their pitch count is
  typically 75-85 after 4+ innings). The 5th inning is characterized by:
    1. Starting pitcher still throwing conservatively to protect pitch count
    2. When SP is pulled mid-5th, the middle reliever enters cold
    3. This is the HIGHEST VARIANCE inning for scoring pace — games can
       either accelerate (middle reliever struggles) OR lock up (SP dominates)
    4. The market prices this as an "upside" moment but our data shows the
       scoring acceleration rarely materializes consistently

  Key evidence: rn [3,4) at inning 4 = 6W/0L (+47.6% ROI)
                rn [3,4) at inning 5 = 2W/5L (-53.5% ROI)
  SAME runs-needed cohort, OPPOSITE performance, just one inning later.

  Inning 5 appears to be where "needs 3-4 more runs" hits a brick wall because:
  - Starting pitcher is protecting final innings
  - Bullpen transition creates volatility, not scoring
  - 4 innings to go is plenty of time for the market to be wrong

TR7 is the first real-data test of the book capture system. Key observation:

  TR7 book captures at signal time:

  Game        ask0   ask@2s  ask@5s  Direction  Notes
  CLE@STL 8.5 0.630  0.630   0.630   STABLE     Thick book ($7k ask depth), held firm

  HOU@SEA 8.5 0.610  0.810   0.890   UP +20¢    Huge surge — market re-priced strongly
  WSH@PIT 7.5 0.800  0.800   0.800   STABLE     $10k bid depth, thick book
  NYM@LAD 7.5 0.680  0.680   0.680   STABLE     Held firm at 0.68

  3 of 5 bets held completely stable. 2 of 5 moved UP (against us).
  ZERO bets showed the "ask retraced after signal" pattern from TR6.

  CRITICAL REVISION OF BUYING_MODEL_1 HYPOTHESIS:

  TR6 data showed wide-spread bets retracing (MIA@DET -13¢, SF@BAL -9¢ at +5s).
  TR7 data shows NO retracing — instead, some bets (HOU@SEA) showed the ask
  continuing to RISE after our signal.

  WHY THE DIFFERENCE?
  Context: HOU@SEA had two bets fired 1 minute apart (dedup window). The 8.5 line
  bet at ask=0.61 → ask jumped to 0.81 at +1s (+20¢). This is NOT a normal
  retrace situation — a second scoring event likely fired the 7.5 bet one minute
  later, and the market was still digesting the first event.

  This means two scenarios exist:
    A. Wide-spread signal where market over-reacted and will retrace (TR6 pattern):
       → Limit order below ask WORKS well (save 5-15¢)
    B. Signal where market is correctly repricing a major event (TR7 HOU@SEA):
       → Limit order below ask would NOT fill — market has moved, we'd miss the bet

  IMPLICATION FOR LIMIT ORDER STRATEGY:
  We cannot know which scenario we're in at signal time. However:
    - If the bid is also rising fast (HOU@SEA: bid went 0.60→0.61→0.61 stayed), the
      bid is lagging the ask surge. A limit at bid+0.65*spread would sit at 0.61 —
      very close to bid, and would never fill because the ask jumped away.
    - If the spread is wide AND the bid is stable (TR6 MIA@DET scenario),
      a limit at mid would fill when the ask retraces toward bid.

  SUGGESTED INDICATOR: "spread velocity" — how fast did the spread CHANGE in the
  last 3 ticks? Rapid spread expansion (ask flying up, bid stable) = scenario B.
  Stable or narrowing spread = scenario A.

  For now: continue collecting data. Need 20+ captures to distinguish patterns.
  The 20% fill rate at spread_factor=0.65 is too low — but may be accurate given
  that most bets in TR7 are "stable" types where the spread is moderate and the
  market doesn't retrace.
