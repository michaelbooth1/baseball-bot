--------------------------------------------
TEST RUN 3 CHANGES
--------------------------------------------

CHANGE 1: MIN CURRENT TOTAL FILTER (fixes pitcher's-duel blindness)
  Problem: SEA@TEX (0-3 through 5 inn) and ATH@NYY (1-2 through 3.5 inn) were
    low-scoring games that the model bet as if they were high-scoring.
  Fix: Add min_current_total = 4. Require that away_score + home_score >= 4
    before placing any bet. If a game has produced fewer than 4 runs by any
    inning, it's likely a pitcher's duel and the model should stay out.
  Expected effect: Blocks SEA@TEX (total=3 at bet) and ATH@NYY (total=3 at bet).
    May filter some LAD@TOR 7.5 (total=1). Small sacrifice on real high-scoring
    games entering early, but eliminates structural pitcher's-duel bets.

CHANGE 2: RUNS PACE FILTER (quantified version of Change 1)
  Problem: Current total vs inning tells us if scoring pace supports the line.
  Fix: Compute expected_pace = (current_total / inning) * 9. If expected_pace
    < (line - 1.5), skip bet. Example: total=3 in inn=4 → pace = 6.75 runs/9inn.
    For line=8.5, expected_pace 6.75 < 7.0 (8.5 - 1.5). Skip.
    A 1.5-run buffer accounts for late-inning acceleration (close games get
    pitching changes, bullpen arms may be weaker).
  Expected effect: Conservative filter that catches games whose scoring pace
    is structurally insufficient to reach the line. Does not block KC@CLE
    (total=4 in inn=4, pace=9 → >=7.0 for 7.5 line, passes). Blocks SEA@TEX
    (total=3 in inn=5, pace=5.4 → <6.0 for 7.5 line, blocked).

CHANGE 3: RAISE min_entry_ask TO 0.55
  Problem: The 0.45-0.55 band produced 6 of 7 losses. The market at 0.45-0.55
    is saying "this is a coin flip at best" — and we need the game to produce
    significant additional scoring. These are contradictory signals.
  Fix: Raise min_entry_ask from 0.45 to 0.55. Only bet when the market is
    pricing the over at >= 55% probability — meaning the current game state
    already has meaningful over momentum.
  Expected effect: Reduces total bets by ~40-50%, cuts all 0.45-0.48 losses.
    SD@PIT 7.5 (0.479) would be filtered — acceptable, as it was a legitimate
    win that barely passed. KC@CLE 7.5 (0.72), BAL@CWS 7.5 (0.73) pass easily.
  Trade-off: Fewer signals, but higher precision expected. At our current bet
    volume this is the right direction.

CHANGE 4: PER-GAME INNING DEDUP (reduce correlated exposure)
  Problem: 60s dedup window catches same-event duplicate signals but does not
    prevent betting on the same game twice across different innings (e.g.
    LAD@TOR: bet at inning 4 and again at inning 7, 85 minutes apart).
  Fix: Add per_game_inning_dedup = True. Track last_bet_inning[game_pk].
    Within the same inning, 60s dedup still applies. Across innings, only
    allow a new bet on game_pk if: (a) current inning > last_bet_inning + 1,
    AND (b) at least 3 innings have elapsed since last bet on this game,
    AND (c) new edge > last_bet_edge + 0.05 (must be meaningfully better).
  Expected effect: Limits correlated exposure. LAD@TOR double-bet (both losses)
    would be reduced to 1 bet. May miss the SD@PIT double-win but acceptable
    — losing the correlated exposure risk matters more over many samples.

CHANGE 5: min_inning = 5 FOR LINES >= 8.5
  Problem: STL@WSH 8.5 entered at Bot4, LAD@TOR 8.5 at Top4, ATH@NYY 8.5
    at Top4. These are very early entries for a high line — 8.5+ runs requires
    a lot more scoring and entering at inning 4 leaves too much uncertainty.
  Fix: Add min_inning_high_line = 5. If line >= 8.5, skip bets placed before
    inning 5. The 7.5 line minimum stays at inning 4.
  Expected effect: Filters ATH@NYY 8.5 (Top4), STL@WSH 8.5 (Bot4), LAD@TOR
    8.5 (Top4). LAD@TOR 8.5 was also bet #9 (late game, inning 7) — that one
    passes. CHC@TB 8.5 (Top5) and SD@PIT 8.5 (Bot7) pass. BAL@CWS 8.5 (Bot5)
    passes but is filtered by Change 3 (ask=0.48 < 0.55).

SECONDARY OBSERVATIONS
--------------------------------------------
  1. GAME SELECTION MATTERS MORE THAN PARAMETER TUNING — The difference between
     a +20% ROI day and a -24% ROI day was driven by which games the model bet,
     not by edge or ask levels. TR1 happened to pick high-scoring games; TR2
     included two low-scoring (pitcher's duel) games by mistake.

  2. MORNING LINES AS PRE-FILTER — Consider using opening total lines as a
     context filter. If the opening line is 7.0 or below (low expected scoring),
     be more conservative or skip. If line is 9.0+, the game is expected to be
     high-scoring — more aggressive. This would require fetching pre-game lines.

  3. TEAM SCORING PACE (future) — A rolling 7-game team scoring average would
     help distinguish structural high-scoring games from flukes. Not yet in scope
     but should be considered for TR4/TR5.

  4. PROFIT TARGET — The SD@PIT bets (wins at 8.5x and 9.17x payout) demonstrate
     that high-edge bets on genuinely high-scoring games produce outsized returns
     (108.77 + 117.39 = $226 profit from 2 bets). The model should be optimized
     to FIND these cases, not to bet everything that meets minimum criteria.
